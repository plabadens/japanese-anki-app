## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

The **design is right and the build is wrong**. The approved contract already describes the app
Pierre describes — capture, one tap on a word, no forms, one grouped busy state, a confirmed send,
sparse haptics, two expressive moments. What shipped on `main` is the *old* app wearing the new
palette: a step tracker, a duplicated sentence, a right-aligned edit link, a "preparation
corrections" form with free-text fields for readings, definition, translation and pitch, checkboxes
for meanings, "Continue" instead of "Review card", a labelled translation block, and no motion or
haptics anywhere in the codebase. The gap is execution, not concept. Two things genuinely new in
Pierre's brief are **not** covered by the contract at all: the **context photograph** (shoot the text,
then shoot the thing the text is on) and the **"almost a game" feel** (sound cues, richer colour).

## 2. The story as stated, and the three real conflicts

Pierre's story, in his words, contains three claims the approved contract currently contradicts. They
must be decided, not quietly smoothed over.

| # | Pierre says | Contract currently says | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | "the definition presented to me with the different possible meanings that I can select" | Resolve silently; show a meaning sheet **only** for genuine ambiguity (Target §6, R08) | Not a real conflict. The *information* must always be visible; the *questionnaire* must not be. What is wrong today is neither: meanings are a permanent multi-select checkbox list (`TargetScreen.kt:183-193`), so the app asks a question even when nothing is ambiguous. |
| B | "I can then confirm, be shown a screen to make sure the information is correct" | Review **is** the confirmation; there is no second screen (state table, R20) | Adopt the contract. A separate confirmation screen would be a fourth screen for a three-decision flow and a second place to notice the same error. Review must therefore be *legible in five seconds* — that is where the effort belongs (§7). |
| C | "different possible meanings that I can select" + "I can then confirm" implies word→definition→confirm as three taps on the normal path | Contract: word selection is the only intentional decision; meaning is automatic unless ambiguity (Target §1-6) | Adopt the contract, and make the *automatic* resolution visible enough that it never feels like a guess: headword, reading, chosen sense, "Change meaning" only when a second viable sense exists. |

Everything else in the story — photograph, tap the word, get the lemma, pronunciation, pitch, send
one complete card, no typing, no fuss — is exactly the approved contract.

## 3. Refined user story (proposed replacement text for the contract's "One user story")

> I am standing in front of something Japanese, and one word in it is worth keeping. I open the app
> and it is already a camera. I take one photo. The app finds the text and shows it to me as a
> sentence I can read — not a puzzle of loose boxes — and I tap the word I do not know. My finger
> lands on the word; the word answers immediately, before I lift my finger, with its dictionary form,
> its reading, and what it means here.
>
> If the word genuinely means more than one thing in this sentence, the app asks me which one, once,
> and then stops asking. If it does not, it does not ask me anything. If the reading or the
> pronunciation is not in the data, it tells me there and then, in one sentence, while I can still
> choose a different word — not after I have committed to a card.
>
> I press Review card. The card assembles in front of me: the sentence with its reading, the
> translation beneath it, the word with its pitch and its real recorded pronunciation, and the
> photograph I took. I can listen to the word. I can add a note or a tag if this one deserves it.
> Everything I need to judge the card is visible without scrolling and without opening anything.
>
> I press Send. The app tells me the truth about what happened, and puts me back at the camera,
> empty, ready for the next one. In a minute I have forgotten the app was involved.
>
> **What the app owes me at each moment**
>
> - **Camera:** it is already pointed at the text; one press; nothing moves or resizes while it
>   works; no choices before the photo exists.
> - **Choose word:** the sentence reads as a sentence; a word answers my tap in under a tenth of a
>   second; the word I chose is the only thing highlighted; nothing I did not touch moves.
> - **Review:** four facts in five seconds — which sentence, which word, which meaning, which
>   translation; one action; no field inventory, no repair menu, no "preparation" vocabulary.
> - **Send:** one press, one honest state at a time, and it either lands or it tells me why not.
>
> **What I never do:** type the sentence I just photographed; pick a dictionary entry from a list;
> correct a reading in a box; choose a deck per card; watch a progress checklist; answer a question
> about something the app could have decided; read an explanation of how the app is built.
>
> **How it should feel:** quick, warm, and physically responsive — a tool with a good mechanism, not
> a form and not a game. The pleasure comes from the thing working the first time and from the
> controls answering my hand, not from points, praise or a completion ceremony.

## 4. Comparator patterns: what to take, what to refuse

| Product | Pattern worth taking | Pattern to refuse |
|---|---|---|
| **Yomitan** (browser popup dictionary) | Lookup is instantaneous and non-modal; the word you point at is the only thing that reacts; card creation is a single confirmation on the same surface. | Hover-only affordance (no touch equivalent), raw dictionary-entry listings with dozens of senses exposed to the learner. |
| **Migaku** (phone OCR → card) | Photograph → interactive text → tap word → one-click card with sentence, audio, image. Proof that the *one-photo, one-word, no-form* loop is viable on a phone. | Its feature surface: separate "study" controls, deck/template choices, on-the-fly sense editing. |
| **jpdb / OCR readers** | Tap-a-word popup, in-place, never leaving the reading surface; "targeted sentence" cards (sentence with the target hidden). | Permanent furigana-on-everything; exposing frequency/"unknown" colouring as the primary visual language. |
| **LingQ** | Tap word → inline highlight + local definition; the text never moves to a side panel. | Known/unknown colour states, counters, streaks — the gamification layer the contract correctly refuses. |
| **Google Lens** | Text in a photograph becomes *selectable text*; region selection only when the image is ambiguous; the capture surface is the navigation. | Region/handle manipulation as a primary interaction; translation-over-text as the product. |
| **AnkiDroid** | The destination is a real API with receipts; the app is honest about what it wrote. | Anything that makes this app a collection editor. |

**The synthesised lesson.** Every product above treats *the found text* as the interface, not a
form field containing the text. This app's Target screen is the exception: it renders the sentence as
a field of separate bubbles. That is the single most important visual finding of this review (§6.1).

## 6. Design judgement: things that are compliant-ish but wrong

### 6.1 The sentence reads as a bag of bubbles (highest-impact issue)

`TargetScreen.kt:307-373` renders each token as a 24 dp-rounded pill with 12 dp horizontal and 8 dp
vertical padding, 8 dp gaps, and punctuation outside the pill but in the same flow. Consequences,
visible in both the mockup and the app:

- The sentence breaks into three or four ragged rows with wide gaps; a short sentence can wrap after
  two words. Line breaks land wherever the pills happen to fit, not at phrase boundaries.
- Punctuation becomes a lone bubble (`。` in its own circle, screenshot 05 / mockup target).
- You cannot **read** the thing you are about to keep; you can only read it word by word.
- Selection changes geometry (#11), so the whole field jumps when you choose.

The contract asks for "compact selectable word units in natural reading order" and "the primary
content". Pills deliver tokens, not reading order. **Proposed amendment:** the sentence renders as
continuous Japanese text at reading size with normal word-and-punctuation spacing; each word is an
invisible hit target inside that line; the selected word gets a tonal fill and an underline drawn
*inside* its line box, with no size change. Chips then exist only in the **meaning** sheet, where the
contract already asks for selectable rows. This keeps 48 dp effective targets (vertical hit slop
shared between adjacent lines), keeps the sentence typographically intact, and makes "tap a word"
obvious because the whole word — not a bubble around it — responds.

**Decided 22 October 2026: adopt inline selectable text.** The fallback (keep the pills, fix the
layout shift, wrap instead of ellipsizing, pull punctuation in) is recorded as rejected. The exact
contract amendment this requires is drafted in Appendix A; the authoritative file is not edited until
that wording is signed off.

### 6.2 The photo can only be a crop of itself

Review shows the photograph in a 144 dp slot with a focal cover-crop (`ReviewCard.kt:153-176,
517-575`). A4-page geometry means you see a horizontal band of a page — enough to recognise, not
enough to remember *where*. `DESIGN.md` §7 authorises the focal crop; the contract says photographs
stay uncropped "where required". This is the seam where Pierre's **context photo** idea fits
naturally (§8).

### 6.3 Status copy is doing the work that layout should do

Review stacks two or three sentences of status above the button. Each is individually reasonable;
together they read as a system explaining itself. The contract's single-blocker rule is not
bureaucracy — it is what stops Review from becoming a diagnostics screen. When several things are
wrong, the app should show the one that blocks *the action the person is trying to take*, and let the
rest surface in order.

### 6.4 Nothing is measured

The contract's central promise is that the local phase never waits. There is no timing evidence in
the repo. Proposal: three budgets, measured on the API-37 emulator and once on hardware — shutter
accept → Target visible **< 1.2 s**; word tap → resolved entry visible **< 100 ms** (p50) / **< 250 ms**
(p95); Review first paint **< 200 ms**. These belong in `SPEC` §13 as numbers rather than adjectives
("responsive").

## 7. Feel: what Pierre asks for, and what is actually missing

Pierre asks for colourful, reactive, low-latency, animated, haptic, with subtle clicks. The contract
already authorises most of it (motion table, haptic table, expressive focal points). The code
implements **none** of it (#17, #18). So the first answer is unglamorous: the delight is designed and
unbuilt.

Design proposals, in order of value:

1. **The selection beat (highest value, no new surface).** On touch-down the word takes a tonal fill
   in ~80 ms with no movement; on commit one very light tick; releasing on a different word moves the
   fill without re-flowing. This single interaction, repeated many times per session, is where the
   app will feel alive or dead.
2. **The card-assembly beat.** On entering Review, the four content groups resolve in their reserved
   space (effects fade, no stagger-per-word, no skeleton shimmer), and the footer's grouped indicator
   gives way to Send. The card *appearing* is the reward; no confetti, no completion screen
   (contract already says this).
3. **The send beat.** Verified delivery: one check treatment + one confirmation haptic + snackbar,
   then Capture with the camera already live. Total < 400 ms of celebration, and the camera is warm
   before the snackbar fades.
4. **Colour — refused (22 October 2026).** Stay vermilion-only: warm paper, ink text, one action per
   screen. The "almost a game" feeling is delivered entirely by reactivity, motion and haptics, not by
   additional colour. The photograph-derived accent is not adopted and must not be implemented.
5. **Sound — refused (22 October 2026).** No app-generated sound. Haptics only, through the platform
   action effects. Any future sound cue is a new contract surface, not a detail.
6. **Reduced motion / haptics off** must still leave every state legible. This is already contracted
   and is the cheap way to prove the feel is not decoration.

## 8. The context photograph: three options

Pierre's idea — close-up of the sign for OCR, then a photo of the house for context — is good, and it
is the difference between a card that teaches a word and a card that remembers a trip. It is also the
one place where an extra feature could re-introduce form-ness.

| Option | What it costs | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A. One photo, as today** | Zero. Card image is the OCR photo, usually an unreadable band of text. | Fine, but the mnemonic is weak. |
| **B. Optional context photo, one extra capture (recommended)** | Draft holds `textPhoto` (OCR source) + optional `contextPhoto`. Review's photo slot gains one quiet action ("Add photo here" / tap the slot while empty) that opens the camera. Export `Image` = context photo if present, else text photo. Viewer shows both. No second OCR, no second sentence, no new screen. | **Deferred 22 October 2026.** Right shape, wrong moment: the daily flow must first match the contract. Do not build it yet; do not design a different version of it either. |
| **C. Parallel two-photo capture mode** | A second camera state, ordering rules, replacement semantics for each slot, re-OCR interplay. | **No.** This is the complexity Pierre already suspected; it violates "one decision per screen" and adds a mode. |

Option B needs a contract amendment because it introduces a photo action in Review (the contract
currently says the photo is replaced only through Capture). Exact proposed wording:
*"Review's photo slot may add or replace an optional **context photograph** — a second, uncropped
photograph that becomes the card image. The OCR source photograph is retained and reachable in the
photo viewer. Adding a context photograph never re-runs OCR and never changes the sentence."*

## 10. Ambiguities in behaviour (code or docs do not answer these)

Each needs a one-line ruling; I propose the answer in the right column.

| # | Ambiguity | Proposed ruling |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does a sentence with **several viable senses** but one obviously dominant reading ask, or resolve? | Resolve if exactly one sense is compatible with the chosen reading and POS; otherwise one sheet. "Dominant by frequency" is not a criterion — the app has no frequency model. |
| 2 | When the **word's reading is unresolved**, can the person still reach Review? | No. Show `reading_unresolved` on Target with the word still selected, so the fix is another word — not a later dead end in Review. |
| 3 | **Repeated identical words** in one sentence: which occurrence is the target? | The occurrence tapped; the highlight is per-span, and the exported `<b>` wraps exactly that span. Already contracted — but the UI must show *which one* was tapped when the word appears twice, e.g. by keeping the selection visibly distinct. |
| 4 | Type mode: is a **photo required**? | No (SPEC §1) — but then the card has no mnemonic. Proposal: keep optional, and offer the same context-photo action in Review so a typed sentence can still get its photo. |
| 5 | **OCR confidence**: how is "several plausible passages" decided? | By number of plausible text blocks after filtering (min characters, punctuation share), not by confidence score. Deterministic and testable; a confidence threshold is not. |
| 6 | Does Review show **furigana on all kanji** or only the target? | All resolved kanji in the sentence (needed to read it), with unresolved kanji visibly marked and Send blocked — already the intent, but nothing prevents a half-ruby sentence from *looking* finished. The blocked state must name the unresolved span. |
| 7 | What happens if the **context photo fails** to process? | Card image falls back to the text photo; the failure is stated in the photo slot only, never as a Send blocker (the image is required for Photo input, and the text photo satisfies it). |
| 8 | **Resume draft** after a context photo was added but the word changed? | Context photo is independent of the sentence; it survives word and meaning changes, and is invalidated only by draft replacement. |
| 9 | Does **"Choose word"** remain enabled when UniDic is not ready? | No — it opens Downloads (already contracted). But today the button is simply disabled with a hint; contract says blocked Choose word **opens Downloads**. |
| 10 | Is the **pitch diagram** shown on Target after selection (contract) or only on Review (screenshots)? | Both, as contracted: resolved headword, reading, chosen meaning and source-backed pitch appear in Target after selection. The screenshot set is stale on this point. |

## 14. Single-scroll alternative: prototype result

A second structure was proposed: **Capture → Card**, one scrollable page where each section appears
after the previous decision (sentence → word → word card → notes → Send), instead of Capture →
Choose word → Review. Both structures were built as identical prototypes — same sentence, palette,
type scale and components, differing only in structure:
`docs/evidence/prototype/flows.html`, captured as `flowA-three-screens.png` and `flowB-one-card.png`.

**Adopted: flow B.** The structure was re-cut in `docs/evidence/prototype/card.html` and
`docs/evidence/prototype/capture.html` with the final composition (hint line, sentence speaker, part of speech, collapse order). One earlier proposal
was withdrawn there: the viewfinder stays exactly as the 22 October contract already specified — an
uncropped 3:4 frame inside the portrait composition — rather than a full-bleed preview with overlaid
chrome. The only new control on Capture is the two-icon mode switch. The implementation-ready text is
[card-flow-contract.md](card-flow-contract.md).

**The finding that decides the question.** In the prototype, A3 (Choose word, resolved) and A4
(Review) are *the same composition*. Everything the third screen adds is a taller photograph, a
repeated word card, and the quiet note/tag actions. So the third screen currently buys a transition
and a Back path — not information. If the third screen is kept, it has to earn its place, and there is
only one thing that can justify it: being the place where preparation is authorised.

**What the single scroll gains**

- No Back-preservation semantics, no Resume draft, no "return to the same selection without
  regenerating", no re-entry rules. State stops being something to restore because there is one
  surface.
- The card assembling top-to-bottom is the progress indication, so progress A stops being a
  compromise and becomes the natural expression of the design.
- Changing your mind is a scroll up, not a navigation decision.

**What it costs**

- The "send without scrolling" acceptance criterion dies and must be replaced by "the action is
  pinned and always legible". A short card leaves a large empty band above a pinned footer; the
  prototype shows this in B2 and B3. Accept it — a primary action that moves with content is worse.
- The page must grow *without moving what the finger is on*: reserved space and insertion at the
  tapped word, never an append at the bottom.
- One more state to name: after a word change, the footer reads **Update card**, not Send.

**Standing judgement:** flow B is the better structure on its own terms, but the choice is being made
from the prototypes rather than from argument. No implementation decision is taken in this document.

## 15. Ruling: paid work begins at sentence commit (22 October 2026)

Translation **and** sentence speech now begin automatically — not at the shutter, and not at the word
tap — at the moment a sentence is **committed to the card** (a single OCR passage proceeding, a
passage chosen from the sheet, or typed text committed). The word's recorded pronunciation begins
when a word is selected.

This is affordable and therefore not a budget question: Chirp 3 HD is metered at $0.00003 per
character, so a 20-character sentence costs about $0.0006, and a hundred discarded captures a day for
a month is about $1.80. Translation is metered on input characters on the same basis. The real
constraints are consent and waste, and both have answers:

1. **The shutter is not the trigger.** A photograph with several passages has no committed sentence
   until one is chosen, so nothing is synthesized for text the person did not choose.
2. **Committing a sentence is consent** to send that sentence's text to Google Text-to-Speech and
   Translation. Photographs still never leave the device, and OCR stays local. `SPEC` §11 must say
   this plainly instead of implying a later authorisation.
3. **Editing the sentence re-synthesizes on commit of the edit**, and the superseded audio becomes
   obsolete. The previous audio is evictable.
4. **No speech when readings are unresolved** — a paid request is never sent for a sentence that
   cannot carry phonetic hints, which is already the hint contract's requirement.
5. **Aborted sentences are evictable** from the 250 MB cache; only active-draft media is protected.
6. **No automatic retry** of a failed paid call. The commit rule authorises the first attempt only.

Consequence for the flow: by the time a word is tapped, the translation and sentence audio usually
already exist, and only the word's recorded pronunciation is still outstanding. In the single-scroll
prototype that is why "Preparing card…" is normally a sub-second state.
