# User story

Japanese learners encounter sentences in books, signs, lessons, and conversation that they want to remember. This app turns one such sentence and one chosen vocabulary item into a complete note in AnkiDroid, without making the learner manage the linguistic or media machinery.

On first use, setup proceeds through dictionary, camera, audio, AnkiDroid, and readiness. A learner may decline camera access and type instead, or defer AnkiDroid connection until export is needed. The dictionary transfer continues in the background, but capture stays closed until it is installed and verified: an app that cannot read the sentence it is given is not usable yet.

In a session, the learner photographs or types a sentence. If the image yields several plausible passages, they choose the intended passage. They select the word worth learning; the app resolves ordinary readings and meanings from its local evidence, and asks about meaning only when the evidence leaves a real choice. It prepares the note's supporting material, then lets the learner inspect it and optionally add personal notes or tags.

The learner explicitly sends the finished note to AnkiDroid. A confirmed write ends the session and leaves the app ready for another sentence. If AnkiDroid's write outcome is uncertain, the app keeps the frozen note and checks the external result without inserting again. Failed work is explained in terms of the operation that failed and offers only a recovery that is safe for that operation.
